What makes an e-commerce customer come back?

A retention analysis on transaction-level data — separating the signals a business can act on from the ones it can only observe.

The question behind the question

"Improve retention" isn't a question — it's a wish. The decision a retention analysis is actually supposed to inform is narrower: where should the next unit of effort go? Onboarding? Discounting? Product breadth? Each has a different cost, and the data has to earn the choice.

So I framed it as: among customers who look identical at the moment of first purchase, what happens next that predicts whether they buy again — and which of those things is under the company's control?

Why this framing matters

Plenty of variables predict retention without being usable. High lifetime spend predicts repeat purchase, but you can't assign someone high lifetime spend. Splitting predictors into observable vs. actionable was the decision that shaped the whole analysis.

Approach

Three stages, each one feeding the next. Add the specifics — which dataset, how many transactions, what date range — once you've locked them.

  • Feature engineering — roll transaction-level data up to the customer level and build features that describe behavior after the first order, not just totals: time to second purchase, category breadth, order value trajectory.
  • Logistic regression — model repeat purchase and validate it properly. I chose logistic over a black-box model on purpose: the coefficients have to be legible to a non-technical reader, because the output of this project is an argument, not a prediction service.
  • Opportunity sizing — for each customer segment, estimate how much retention is actually available to win, so the recommendation is ranked by impact rather than by effect size.
Deliverables

A two-page product requirements document and a five-slide executive deck, ranking retention initiatives by expected impact against implementation cost — each with the metric that would tell you it worked. The notebook is the evidence; the deck is the deliverable.
